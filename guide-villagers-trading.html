Villagers and Trading

Villagers and Trading

Guide

Trading is the most reliable economy in the game. Mining for diamonds is luck; a well-run trading hall gives you enchanted gear, tools and building blocks on demand.

Professions and Job Sites
An unemployed villager takes a profession from the job site block you place near it, and there are thirteen to choose from.

  • Armorer — Buys coal, iron and diamonds, and sells armour outright — iron, chainmail and, at master rank, enchanted diamond pieces. Also a source of shields and bells. Job site: Blast Furnace
  • Butcher — Buys raw meat and crops such as sweet berries, potatoes and carrots, and sells cooked meat, rabbit stew and dried kelp blocks. Job site: Smoker
  • Cartographer — Buys paper and glass panes, and sells empty and filled maps, banner patterns, and the explorer maps that lead to ocean monuments and woodland mansions. Job site: Cartography Table
  • Cleric — Buys rotten flesh and gold ingots — the easiest way to turn mob-farm junk into emeralds — and sells redstone, lapis lazuli, glowstone, ender pearls and bottles o’ enchanting. Job site: Brewing Stand
  • Farmer — Buys wheat, potatoes, carrots and pumpkins, which turns any crop farm into income, and sells bread, pies, cookies, cake, golden carrots and glistering melon slices. Job site: Composter
  • Fisherman — Buys string, coal and raw fish, and sells cooked fish, campfires, boats and an enchanted fishing rod. Job site: Barrel
  • Fletcher — Buys sticks, flint, feathers and string, and sells arrows, bows, crossbows and tipped arrows. The stick trade is the cheapest early emerald in the game. Job site: Fletching Table
  • Leatherworker — Buys leather, rabbit hide and turtle scutes, and sells dyed leather armour, leather horse armour and, at master rank, a saddle. Job site: Cauldron
  • Librarian — The most valuable villager: buys paper, books and ink sacs, and sells enchanted books at every rank, including Mending. Also sells bookshelves, lanterns, glass, name tags, clocks and compasses. Job site: Lectern
  • Mason — Buys clay and stone, and sells bricks, terracotta, glazed terracotta, quartz and polished stone. The one to keep if you build in bulk. Job site: Stonecutter
  • Shepherd — Buys wool and dye, and sells wool, carpet, beds, banners and paintings in every colour. Job site: Loom
  • Toolsmith — Buys coal, iron and diamonds, and sells stone, iron and enchanted diamond tools, along with bells. Job site: Smithing Table
  • Weaponsmith — Buys coal, iron and diamonds, and sells iron and enchanted diamond axes and swords, along with bells. Job site: Grindstone
  • Nitwit — Wears a green coat and never takes a job, whatever block you place. It has no trades at all — breed it out or move it on. Job site: none
  • Unemployed — Any villager with no job site yet. Place a job block within range and it takes that profession; it is also the state a villager returns to if you break the block before its first trade locks in. Job site: none

Break the block before the villager's first trade is locked in and it becomes unemployed again, ready to take a new profession. This is how you fish for the trade you actually want.

Levels
Villagers advance through five ranks as you trade with them: novice, apprentice, journeyman, expert and master. Each rank unlocks new offers, so the good trades only appear after you have used the early ones.

Restocking
A villager restocks up to twice a day, but only by working at its job site block. A villager with no access to its block sells out permanently, which is the most common reason a trading hall stops working.

Prices
Prices move. Trading repeatedly with one villager raises its prices; the Hero of the Village effect, earned by defeating a raid, lowers them sharply for a while.
The largest discount comes from curing a zombie villager with a splash potion of weakness and a golden apple. A cured villager offers dramatically cheaper trades permanently, and it remembers each cure, so curing the same villager twice reduces prices further.

Trades Worth Seeking
Librarians sell enchanted books, including Mending, which is the single most valuable enchantment in the game and cannot be obtained from an enchanting table reliably. Farmers buy crops for emeralds, turning a wheat farm into income. Cartographers sell maps to woodland mansions and ocean monuments. Toolsmiths and armorers sell enchanted gear outright.

Protecting Your Villagers
Villagers are fragile. Light the whole area so nothing spawns inside, keep beds for them so they breed and respawn, and let an iron golem patrol. On hard difficulty a zombie converts rather than kills a villager, which means a cure and a discount, but only if you reach it in time.

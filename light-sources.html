Light Sources

Light Sources

Hard

Light sources in Minecraft are essential for preventing mob spawns, enabling plant growth, and creating visually appealing environments. This guide covers various light sources, their brightness levels, and advanced lighting strategies to keep your builds both functional and beautiful.

Brightness Levels in Minecraft
Minecraft uses a 0–15 brightness scale for light sources. Since version 1.18, most hostile mobs require a block light level of 0 to spawn, so any light at all is enough to keep an area clear. Before 1.18 the threshold was 7 or lower, which is why older guides and torch grids recommend far more lighting than you actually need. Some spawns follow their own rules regardless of light, including Nether fortress mobs and mobs produced by spawners.

Common Light Sources
Torches: The most basic light source, providing a brightness level of 14. They can be placed on walls or floors, and they never burn out.
Glowstone: Provides brightness level 15, the highest among natural light sources. It is a solid block that emits significant light and is often used in high-need areas.
Lanterns: Similar to torches but with a brightness level of 15. They can be hung from ceilings or attached to walls and are more decorative than torches.
Jack o'Lanterns: Provide brightness level 15 and feature a unique pumpkin face design. Carving a pumpkin and adding a torch is the only way to make one.
Redstone Lamps: Emit brightness level 15 when powered and turn off when unpowered. They require redstone components to activate and can be controlled with switches.
Sea Lanterns: Provide brightness level 15 and have a beautiful underwater glow effect. Made from prismarine and glowstone, they are excellent for underwater builds.
End Rods: Provide brightness level 14 and have a unique, slender design. They are perfect for vertical lighting and decorative applications.
Magma Blocks: Provide brightness level 3 and damage any entity standing on them, which makes them useful as a hazard as well as a dim light source.
Campfires: Provide brightness level 15, the same as glowstone, and can cook food without fuel. They are excellent for outdoor lighting as well as cooking.

Advanced Lighting Strategies
Underground Lighting: Use a combination of glowstone and torches to create well-lit caves while maintaining an aesthetic appearance.
Perimeter Lighting: Because any non-zero light level now blocks spawning, a torch reaches roughly 14 blocks in every direction. Spacing torches well inside that range leaves no dark gaps.
Vertical Lighting: Use end rods, chains, or hanging lanterns to light up multi-level structures from top to bottom.
Decorative Lighting: Combine functional lighting with architectural elements to create ambient and artistic light effects in your builds.
Redstone-Controlled Lighting: Use levers, buttons, or sensors to create dynamic lighting systems that turn on only when needed.

Resource-Efficient Lighting
Lava: Emits brightness level 15, so lava pools light their surroundings well, but it destroys items and sets entities alight. Use it only where it is properly contained.
Soul Campfires: Provide brightness level 10, which is dimmer than a regular campfire rather than brighter. They also damage entities that stand in them and repel piglins.

Important Tips
Any light level above 0 prevents hostile spawns in current versions, so a sparse scattering of torches is enough.
Use glowstone instead of torches in well-lit areas where you want maximum brightness.
Hanging lanterns provide better coverage than wall-mounted torches.
Underwater lighting needs blocks that survive being submerged: sea lanterns and glowstone both work, and glow lichen can be waterlogged for a dimmer level 7. Prismarine and coral blocks emit no light of their own, despite their appearance.
Campfires can be used both for cooking and decorative lighting in outdoor areas.

Light levels and spawning rules on this page reflect Minecraft 1.18 and later.
